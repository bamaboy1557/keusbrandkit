Chapter 01
Typography
KEUS uses two typefaces — and only two. One for big bold moments, one for everything else.
Think of fonts like voices. KEUS has a formal, elegant voice for headlines and a clear, readable voice for body copy. Mixing them correctly is what makes KEUS material feel premium and coherent — whether it's a billboard or a WhatsApp message.
1.1 — Primary Font Headlines · Hero Text · Campaign Titles · Editorial Pull Quotes
✦
What this font is for

Playfair Display is KEUS's signature voice. Use it for the biggest, most important text on any piece — the headline on a poster, the title on a reel, the hero statement on a brochure. It should feel like something worth reading slowly. Never use it for body copy or small text — it was not designed for that and will look wrong below 18pt.

Playfair Display
Regular Italics Bold Bold Italics
Rules to follow — every time
1
Bold and Bold Italic are exceptions, not defaults. Only use them on large-format print (banners, hoardings) where extra emphasis is genuinely needed. For all standard applications, Regular or Italic is correct.
2
The capital "Q" must always be modified. In Playfair Display, the capital Q has a tail that crosses the letter and looks too similar to the capital O. Every time you type a Q in this font, use the Shear Tool or Shape Builder in Illustrator to angle the tail. This is a non-negotiable brand consistency rule — it applies to every medium, every format, every vendor.
3
Use on white or clear backgrounds whenever possible. If the background is coloured or photographic, switch to the white version of the font. If the background is very dark, use the white font. If in doubt — use white or black, never a colour.
1.2 — Secondary Font Subheadings · Body Copy · Captions · UI Text · Website · Print Body
✦
What this font is for

Source Sans Pro is the workhorse. It handles everything that isn't a headline — descriptions, specifications, captions, website body text, button labels, form fields, legal text, and any copy that needs to be read comfortably at small sizes. It comes in many weights, from very thin to bold, giving you flexibility without ever needing a third font.

1.3 — Type Hierarchy How the fonts work together on a page
✦
Why hierarchy matters

Hierarchy is the order in which a reader's eye travels through a page. Think of it like a newspaper — the big headline pulls you in, the subheading gives you context, the body text tells you the whole story. If everything is the same size, nothing stands out. The sizes and weights below are the exact ratios KEUS uses — follow them and the layout will feel considered and premium automatically.

Chapter 02 — 03
Colour Palettes
KEUS uses exactly four colours — and so does Ardeo. They share the same palette. What changes is how it's applied.
Think of the KEUS brand as a warm, sunlit interior — beige walls, a dark wood table, crisp white linen. Ardeo is the same room at night — dark, dramatic, sophisticated. Same four colours. Completely different mood. The brand canvas (background colour) is what separates them — not separate palettes.
How the two brands look side by side
KEUS Automation lives on light backgrounds. Ardeo by Keus lives on dark backgrounds. This is the most important visual distinction between the two brands.

KEUS Automation — the light brand

KEUS Automation materials should feel warm, refined, and approachable — like walking into a well-designed home. Beige is the dominant colour and should cover at least half of every design. White gives it airiness. Brown grounds it. Black adds precision and contrast. If you find yourself reaching for any colour outside this list, stop — it's not KEUS.

Ardeo by Keus — the dark brand

Ardeo is the lighting sub-brand. Its materials should feel dramatic, precise, and architectural — like a perfectly lit gallery at night. Black is the dominant canvas. Beige and white appear as light against darkness — just as Ardeo's luminaires reveal architecture through light. Same four colours as KEUS, completely different character. Never mix Ardeo dark-canvas materials with KEUS light-canvas materials in the same campaign.

Logo Assets
Logo Usage & Downloads
The KEUS logo is the single most recognisable brand element. How you use it — and where — matters enormously.
The logo comes in two versions: Black and White. You choose based on the background it sits on. There is no colour version, no gradient version, and no version with a drop shadow. If you receive a request to modify the logo in any way not shown here, refer back to this document.
✦
The golden rule of logo usage

The logo must always be legible. If you cannot read "KEUS" clearly, the logo is wrong for that background. When in doubt — use the black logo on light backgrounds, white logo on dark backgrounds. Never stretch, rotate, recolour, add effects to, or recreate the logo from scratch. Always use the approved SVG files provided here.

Correct logo usage — quick rules
✓ Always do this
→Use the SVG file provided — it scales perfectly at any size without quality loss
→Maintain clear space around the logo equal to the height of the "K" on all sides
→Use black logo on light/beige/white backgrounds
→Use white logo on dark/black/dark-photo backgrounds
→Keep the logo at its original proportions — always scale uniformly
✗ Never do this
→Recolour the logo in any colour other than black or white
→Add drop shadows, glows, gradients, or outlines to the logo
→Stretch, squash, or rotate the logo
→Place the logo on a busy, patterned, or low-contrast background
→Recreate the logo in a different font or redraw it from memory

SVG format explained: SVG (Scalable Vector Graphic) is the gold-standard format for logos. Unlike JPG or PNG, an SVG file will look perfectly sharp at any size — from a business card to a building. If your software doesn't accept SVG, ask for a PNG export at the specific size you need — do not attempt to scale a small PNG up.
